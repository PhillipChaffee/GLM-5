Abstract
Decoder hidden states from different layers of large lan-
guage models (LLMs) often encode complementary in-
formation. However, existing speculative decoding meth-
ods typically rely on a small set of target-model layers
chosen using fixed, position-based heuristics. Selecting
an optimal subset of layers is inherently a combinatorial
problem, making exhaustive evaluation infeasible even
for moderately deep models. In this work, we present a
principled framework for multi-layer hidden-state selec-
tion for speculative decoding based on two complemen-
tary criteria: informativeness, measured through matrix-
entropy analysis, and diversity, quantified using centered
kernel alignment (CKA). We investigate layer-wise Shan-
non entropy, aggregate entropy under hidden-state con-
catenation, and pairwise CKA dynamics across depth,
revealing distinct geometric and informational compart-
ments within decoder representations. Motivated by
these findings, we propose entropy and CKA-based layer
selection algorithms that favor hidden-state sets that are
both highly informative and minimally redundant. As
part of this framework, we introduce token-entropy fil-
tering, which removes hidden states associated with low
predictive-entropy tokens, thereby improving the quality
of layer-selection statistics. In EAGLE3-style specula-
tive decoding, our selected sets outperform fixed base-
line choices, including the default [2, N//2, N− 3] rule,
while providing a clearer explanation of why some de-
faults can remain competitive. Our results provide a prac-
tical recipe for multi-layer hidden-state selection for spec-
ulative decoding, insights into the geometry and informa-
tion in LLM decoder states, and a foundation for extend-
ing similar analysis to related applications.
1 Introduction
Hidden states in decoder-only LLMs capture the pro-
gressive transformation of token representations through
depth. While prior studies analyze individual layers
through rank, sparsity, and entropy, modern inference
pipelines increasingly use multiple target-model hidden
states jointly. In speculative decoding methods such as
EAGLE3, selected target-model layers are fused and pro-
vided to the draft model. However, in practice these lay-
ers are usually selected by simple fixed rules (e.g., po-
sitional heuristics) rather than an explicit objective that
balances informativeness and redundancy.
This creates both a practical and scientific gap. Prac-
tically, multi-layer selection is combinatorial. For a 36-
layer model, choosing only three layers yields thousands
of combinations, and the search space grows rapidly for
larger tuples. Scientifically, it remains unclear which
layer combinations are jointly informative versus geo-
metrically redundant. As a result, default position-based
heuristics (uniform selection with the initial and final lay-
ers offset) may perform well in some regimes without
providing a principled explanation of when or why they
work.
In this work, we view multi-layer hidden-state selec-
tion through the lenses of information maximization and

2 Related Work
Layer specialization and redundancy in transform-
ers. Prior work shows that transformer layers are not
uniformly useful and that representational redundancy is
often concentrated in middle layers. Transformer layer-
function studies report that many middle layers are par-
tially interchangeable, while early and late layers re-
main more functionally distinct [1]. Related pruning/ac-
celeration work similarly finds that substantial middle-
layer computation can be removed with limited quality
loss [2]. Representation-geometry analyses also suggest
structured evolution of hidden states across depth [3, 4].
These studies motivate transition-aware reasoning: the
depth axis can be divided into coherent compartments
separated by sharp representational changes. To algorith-
mically identify these compartments, we introduce CKA-
transition diagnostics.
Spectral and rank-based analyses. A complementary
line of research studies rank and spectral structure for
finetuning and compression. Adaptive rank-allocation
methods for LoRA finetuning [5] demonstrate that rank
requirements vary by layer/module and should not be
treated uniformly [6, 7]. Other methods use low-rank
approximations of transformer components for eﬀiciency
[8]. Random-matrix and singular-value analyses further
show that spectral tails and non-dominant directions can
materially affect behavior [9]. However, these works pri-
marily deal with over-parameterization and compression
- they do not address the problem of selection of sets of
activations.
Speculative decoding and hidden-state conditioning.
Recent speculative decoding frameworks such as EAGLE
[10, 11] and DFlash [12] condition draft models on se-
lected target-model hidden states. In current practice,
those target layers are usually chosen by fixed, position-
based heuristics (e.g., [N−1] or [2, N//2, N−3]) which

may lead to redundant representations. Our work ad-
dresses this gap through ELSI, which maximizes infor-
mation and diversity through matrix entropy and CKA-
transition structure signals for principled multi-layer se-
lection. We further introduce beam-overlap diagnostics
to explain when and why position-based baseline rules
still remain competitive.
Positioning of this work. Relative to prior layer-
analysis and rank-based compression literature, our con-
tribution is to jointly optimize hidden-layer sets for down-
stream draft conditioning: (i) quantify set informative-
ness via entropy of fused representations with ELSI, (ii)
control redundancy via CKA-transition-guided expan-
sion with ELSI-C, and (iii) evaluate practical gains in
EAGLE3-style speculative decoding with interpretable
diagnostics.

4 Method
We propose a three-stage layer-selection pipeline for
speculative decoding: (i) token-entropy filtering within
hidden states, (ii) hidden-state aggregation to build layer
representatives, (iii.a) entropy-driven beam search for in-
formative multi-layer sets (ELSI), and (iii.b) transition-
aware CKA expansion for diversity-oriented refinement
(ELSI-C). This section formalizes each stage and the
evaluation protocol used in our experiments.

4.2 Hidden State Collection and
Block Averaging
For each model and calibration prompt, we collect hidden
states and apply token filtering ( h(Ω)l ). We then partition
the token axis into non-overlapping blocks of sizeB = 32
and average across blocks/samples to obtain layer repre-
sentatives
Zl∈ RB×d, l = 0, . . . , N− 1. (11)
These{Zl} are used for entropy/CKA analyses and layer-
set scoring. In our experiments, we use 200 calibration
samples from ShareGPT [15].
4.3 Matrix Entropy-based Expansion
(ELSI)
ELSI performs beam search with matrix entropy of con-
catenated hidden states as the expansion criterion. We
use Zl to denote the layer representatives from Eq. (11)
and ¯ZL ≜ concat({Zl}l∈L, feature dim).
ELSI is detailed in Algorithm 1 which begins with
entropy-based singleton seeding ([S1]) and then performs
beam expansion over set sizes ([S2]). At each depth, can-
didates are deduplicated ([S3]) and pruned by entropy
score ([S4]) before selecting the best size- m set ([S5]).

4.4 ELSI-C: Entropy-Initialized CKA
Expansion
ELSI-C reuses entropy initialization and entropy scoring,
but restricts each expansion to a transition-aware candi-
date pool from conditional CKA. Algorithm 2 first ini-
tializes the beam using entropy ([C1]). For each beam
member, it computes a transition score for each candi-
date using CKA ([C2]). Candidate layers with the high-
est transition scores are then kept for each beam ([C3]).
Next, candidate expansions are aggregated with dedupli-
cation and the beam is pruned based on entropy ([C4]).
The final size- m set is selected as the top beam element
([C5]).

5.4 CKA compartments and transi-
tions
Figure 5 shows pairwise CKA with transition bound-
aries derived from Eq. 8 overlaid. The transition
analysis reveals clear depth-wise representational com-
partments. Qwen3-4B-IT has more transitions than
Llama3.1-8B-IT, showing more variation as we go
deeper into the network, with two large compartments in
the middle. Llama3.1-8B-IT on the other hand has sim-
ilar CKA throughout except for the first and last layers.
From this perspective, EAGLE2-like methods which
only used last layer hidden-states might miss out on other
high information layers. EAGLE3 which selects layers
following [2, N//2, N− 3] selects layers in different
compartments, thus resulting in improved performance
over EAGLE2, though it might still be sub-optimal by
having overlapping information.

When neighboring layers encode similar information, the
choice of layers matters correspondingly less, and a uni-
formly spaced selection already performs well. Framing
layer selection through ELSI thus allows us to identify
these differing regimes across models: where represen-
tations are diverse, selection results in significant bene-
fits, and where they are redundant, it instead allows for a
smaller layer-set selection.
6 Conclusion
We studied multi-layer hidden-state selection for spec-
ulative decoding through the lens of information max-
imization and geometric diversity, moving beyond
fixed, positional heuristics. Our analysis of de-
coder representations across Qwen3-4B-Instruct and
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct revealed consistent struc-
tural signals: a non-uniform entropy profile across lay-
ers (the activation wave), non-uniform entropy gains
with layer concatenation, and CKA transition boundaries
that indicate distinct representational compartments. To-
gether, these observations motivate joint selection of
layer sets to maximize information and minimize redun-
dancy, instead of relying on hand-crafted layer indices.
Building on these signals, we introduced ELSI and ELSI-
C as practical algorithms to select layer combinations for
typical model sizes. We further enhanced these algo-
rithms by introducing token-entropy filtering to reduce
cross-prompt entropy variance, improving the stability
of selection statistics. Empirically, we demonstrate (i)
choice of layers matters more than the number of lay-
ers, and (ii) layer selection can result in sets that out-
perform EAGLE2 and EAGLE3 baselines. We further
provide analysis of why currently used layer combina-
tions do perform well in practice, thus enhancing the in-
terpretability of current speculative decoding techniques.
Overall, this work provides a practical and interpretable
framework for multi-layer hidden-state selection in spec-
ulative decoding. This framework is designed to be gen-
eral, with the potential for future work that experiments
with a broader variety of target models and draft-model
conditioning strategies. Further, the selection of sets of
layers may find application in a broader range of tasks
beyond speculative decoding including embedding tasks
and interpretability, and we encourage these as future di-
rections for exploration.
