# Token-budget batching for the hidden-state capture pass: prior art and design

Research supporting the cache-stage batching work under [Train the GLM-5.3-Flash drafter via DeepSpec on Modal, end to end](https://github.com/PhillipChaffee/GLM-5/issues/23) (map #1). Compiled 2026-10-12 by web research (serving-engine source code and docs, offline-training samplers, EAGLE/DeepSpec cache scripts) plus code-verified reading of our own capture stage. Motivation: our cache stage uses a fixed conversation batch size (proven safe at 2; 16 OOMs), and the question was whether serving-style admission control — admit work only when its worst-case memory fits — can replace it.

## Answer in one paragraph

**Token-budget batching is the standard admission mechanism in every major serving stack, and the prefill-only analog of our capture pass exists in production (TRT-LLM's hidden-state capture for EAGLE-3 training, batched by the engine's own token budget) — while our upstreams use neither: EAGLE v1 captures one conversation per forward, DeepSpec uses a fixed count with no budget.** The mechanism that fits our hooks-based capture is fairseq-style greedy token-budget packing over a length-sorted stream, admitting whole conversations (never chunked prefill — splitting one conversation across forwards breaks per-conversation capture), with the budget measured by probe at `max_length` rather than derived, and paired with a conversation-count cap because at p50 = 277 tokens a pure token budget would admit hundreds of conversations into one forward and the 7 hook-pinned capture streams cannot absorb that. Batch 2 stays as the safe floor and count cap; the token budget supersedes it as the operating point.

## 1. How the serving engines budget a forward pass

- **vLLM (V1 scheduler)**: admission bounded by `max_num_batched_tokens` ("maximum number of tokens that can be processed in a single iteration"); on >=160 GB cards (B200/B300) the default is 16,384 for both offline and server. A second, separate budget governs KV admission (`allocate_slots` + a `watermark` fraction kept free). `long_prefill_token_threshold` caps a long prompt's per-iteration share and "is not applied when the request is the only one in the batch". <https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/vllm/config/scheduler.py,> `vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py`, `vllm/engine/arg_utils.py`
- **TGI**: batches to `--max-batch-total-tokens` — "for max_batch_total_tokens=1000, you could fit 10 queries of total_tokens=100 or a single query of 1000 tokens" — and **auto-infers the budget from measured VRAM** (subtract model weights, divide remaining VRAM by per-token KV+MLP cost, take as large as possible). A separate `--max-batch-prefill-tokens` bounds the prefill operation specifically ("this operation takes the most memory and is compute bound"). `launcher/src/main.rs`
- **SGLang**: two compute budgets plus a memory budget — `--max-prefill-tokens` (default 16,384) and `--chunked-prefill-size` (default 8,192) — enforced in `PrefillAdder`, which charges both budgets per admission while reserving page-ceiled KV from the allocator's memory budget. `python/sglang/srt/managers/schedule_policy.py`, `arg_groups/fields/schedule.py`
- **TensorRT-LLM**: in-flight batching requires packed inputs; `max_num_tokens` (default 8,192) is exactly the activation-size knob — "max_num_tokens affects workspace buffer sizes... and one of the matrix multiplication dimensions". The scheduler admits whole requests until the budget is exhausted. **Prefill-only analog**: the `trtllm-serve embeddings` dynamic batcher dispatches a forward as soon as `--max_batch_size` is reached, the next request would exceed the `--max_num_tokens` budget, or a `--max_queue_delay` hold window elapses; its offline `llm.encode()` fast path is "a single forward pass per batch, no KV cache, sampler, or decode loop". <https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/features/paged-attention-ifb-scheduler.html,> /features/embeddings.html
- **Production prior art for our exact workload**: TRT-LLM's `SaveHiddenStatesDecodingConfig` ("SaveState" decoding mode) captures target hidden states for EAGLE-3 drafter training through the serving engine itself — `eagle3_layers_to_capture` (intermediate layers + `-1` for the final post-norm state), `output_directory`, `write_interval`, files containing `input_ids` + `hidden_state` + `aux_hidden_states` — batched by the engine's own IFB scheduler with the `maxNumTokens` budget. NVIDIA's answer to "capture hidden states at scale" is "let the token-budgeted serving scheduler do it." <https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/llm-api/reference/SaveHiddenStatesDecodingConfig.html>
- **Why variance killed fixed counts historically**: Orca (OSDI'22) introduced iteration-level scheduling; Anyscale's benchmark measured 23x (vLLM continuous batching), 8x (TGI), 4x (optimized static) over naive static batching on a high-variance decode workload — "as the variance increases, naive static batching's performance plummets." <https://www.anyscale.com/blog/continuous-batching-llm-inference>

## 2. Offline prior art (training-side batchers)

- **fairseq `batch_by_size`** is the canonical offline token-budget batcher: `max_tokens` per batch over length-sorted indices, greedy packing, Cython fast path; the last batch may be padded to a required multiple. <https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairseq> `fairseq/data/data_utils.py`
- **SpeechBrain `DynamicBatchSampler`**: "specifying a max_batch_length which is the upper limit for the sum of the length of examples in a batch... Should be chosen based on your GPU memory", with a `max_batch_ex` count cap and per-bucket batch sizes `max_batch_length / bucket_boundary[i]` (token-constant per bucket). `speechbrain/dataio/sampler.py`
- **HF Trainer**: `group_by_length` is fixed-count with length grouping — and deliberately puts the longest element in the first batch "so that an OOM happens sooner rather than later". The newer `BatchRebalanceSampler` states the cost model formally: **`cost(bs, max_len) = bs*max_len + c*bs*max_len^2`** — "the linear term captures linear-layer compute and padding waste; the quadratic term captures attention's O(L^2) cost" — and produces variable-size micro-batches balanced by cost. This is the formal statement of why a fixed count of 16 with one 7k-token conversation in a p50-277 corpus OOMs. `src/transformers/trainer_pt_utils.py`
- **sentence-transformers `encode()`**: the classic length-sorted offline pipeline (sort, batch with fixed count, unsort results). `sentence_transformers/model.py::_inference`
- **torchtune `PackedDataset`**: greedy packing into fixed token blocks with block-diagonal masks (attention isolation between packed samples) — the training-side analog of whole-sequence packing. `torchtune/datasets/_packed.py`

## 3. What our upstreams do (code-verified)

- **EAGLE v1**: `eagle/ge_data/allocation.py` carves the corpus into contiguous per-GPU ranges; `ge_data_all_vicuna.py` loops **batch size = 1** — one conversation per forward, `torch.save` per conversation. No batching, no budget. (Current EAGLE main removed `ge_data` entirely in favor of SGLang's SpecForge for EAGLE-3.)
- **DeepSpec upstream**: `scripts/data/prepare_target_cache.py` — per-rank contiguous range, **fixed-count** `DataLoader(batch_size=local_batch_size)` (default 32), hooks into a dict, async shard writer, one process per GPU. **No dynamic token budget anywhere.** Our GLM mirror differs only in single-process `device_map="auto"`, mHC stream folding, final-norm re-application, `logits_to_keep=1`, and align-device copies.
- **DSpark paper** (local `papers/DSpark-2607.05147.txt` lines 796–807): trains on "anchor-bounded sequence packing... via token-level attention indices" — token-budget-adjacent packing, but on the drafter-training side after the cache exists, not during capture.

## 4. Design for our capture pass

- **Mechanism**: fairseq-style greedy token-budget packing over a length-sorted stream, admitting **whole conversations** (budget >= `max_length` = 4096 so the truncated tail always fits one-per-forward). Sorted streaming makes the padded peak per forward ≈ the budget (pad waste ≈ 0), so the measured budget directly bounds the activation peak. Strict monotonic ordering is available to a capture pass (shards become monotonic length ranges) — a simplification training pipelines deliberately give up (HF keeps intra-megabatch randomness only because training needs it).
- **Never chunked prefill**: vLLM/TGI/TRT-LLM chunked prefill splits one sequence across forwards carrying KV cache; our hooks fire per layer per forward, so a split would need KV carry plus capture stitching. Whole-sequence budgets only.
- **Pair the token budget with a count cap**: every serving stack does (`max_num_seqs`, `max_batch_ex`, `prefill_max_requests`). At p50 = 277 tokens a 16–32k budget would admit ~60–120 conversations into one forward; our hooks pin 7 streams and dynamic shapes this large are untested. The existing batch-size flag survives as this cap.
- **Measure the budget, don't derive it**: TGI auto-infers from VRAM; the offline analog is a probe run at synthetic `max_length` minus explicit headroom — exactly how "safe at 2" was established. The budget must account for: (1) the activation peak ∝ padded batch tokens at the batch's max length; (2) the **7 hook-pinned capture streams** — GLM's mHC makes these 4-D `[B,S,N,D]` tensors alive from hook fire until `fold_streams` completes, ~7x a single-stream pin; (3) align-device copies (pinned H2D/D2D copies riding streams that hold sources alive concurrently with the next forward); (4) the async shard-writer queue (`max_queue_size=local_batch_size*4` holds up to 4 batches on their way to disk).
- **Interaction with the resumable-build design**: slices become ranges of the length-sorted order (contiguous, monotonic), with manifests per slice as already planned; length-sorting the corpus once up front is a cheap CPU step.

## 5. Expected speedup and the open gap

Batch 2 runs ~1.7k padded tokens/forward at the measured ~219 tok/s; a 16–32k budget is a **10–20x jump in tokens-per-forward** — but the realized speedup depends on where the workload flips from memory/overhead-bound to compute-bound, and no public benchmark pins token-budget vs fixed-count offline capture at matched memory. That number is ours to measure: one probe sweep over a few budgets (the cache-bucketing throughput experiment) converts this design into the cost model's re-anchored cache-stage line.

## Gaps

- Whether our collator pads to in-batch longest or to `max_length=4096` is unverified (parser is read only through the mask filter); the collator's pad call must be confirmed before the budget probe is designed.
- tf.data `bucket_by_sequence_length` semantics cited via SpeechBrain's docstring only; SpecForge's batching internals not examined; TRT-LLM `DSparkDecodingConfig` details not fetched.
- No public A/B of token-budget vs fixed-count offline capture at matched memory (see §5).
